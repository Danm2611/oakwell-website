Bradford's houses were built from the stone under them, and a lot of the older roofs are stone slate: heavy, graded from large at the eaves to small at the ridge, and laid on roofs that were framed to carry the weight. Re-covering a stone slate roof properly means sorting and re-laying the slates by size, on new battens and membrane, and it is a job we enjoy. Where stone slate has been replaced with concrete tile in the past we can go back to slate, or re-lay in a slate-effect tile that keeps the look at a lower cost.

The stone terraces of Great Horton, Manningham, Heaton and Eccleshill have the same chimney issues as every Victorian street: tall shared stacks, soft mortar and lead flashings that were last touched decades ago. We repoint, rebuild and re-lead them, or take redundant stacks down below the roofline.

Further out, the semis and bungalows of Wibsey, Clayton and Thornton are concrete tile with flat-roofed garages, and the newer estates around Apperley Bridge and Menston are where our new-build slate work comes in.
